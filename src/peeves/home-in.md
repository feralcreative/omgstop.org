## The two words

**Home in** comes from homing pigeons and later homing missiles—moving progressively closer to a target. It always takes *in* or *in on*.

**Hone** means to sharpen on a whetstone. You hone a blade, or a skill. It doesn't take *in*, because sharpening isn't directional.

## The honest caveat

*Hone in* is common enough that several dictionaries now record it as a variant, and it's been drifting that way since the 1960s. It's still the one people notice, and *home in* is never wrong.

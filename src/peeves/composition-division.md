title: "composition and division"
category: Logical fallacies

summary: "True of the parts is not automatically true of the whole. Or the reverse."
rule: "What holds for a <em>piece</em> need not hold for the thing it's part of."
snark: "Every word here is short, so this is a short page."
examples:
  - wrong: "\"Every component is fast, so the system is fast.\""
    right: "\"Where does the time actually go?\""
    note: "Systems are made of interactions, and those are where the time goes."
---

Two directions of the same mistake. **Composition** goes part-to-whole: every ingredient is delicious, so the meal is. **Division** goes whole-to-part: the company is profitable, so this team is.

Some properties do transfer and some don't, and which is which isn't obvious. The fallacy is assuming rather than checking.

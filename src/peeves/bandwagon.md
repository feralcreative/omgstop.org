title: "bandwagon"
category: Logical fallacies
order: 107
summary: "Lots of people believe it. Lots of people have been wrong before."
rule: "Popularity measures <em>how many</em> believe it, not whether it's true."
snark: "Everyone else has read this already, so it must be right."
aliases:
  - ad-populum
  - appeal-to-popularity
examples:
  - wrong: "\"Everyone's moving to this framework.\""
    right: "\"What does it do better?\""
    note: "Adoption can follow marketing as easily as merit."
---

Also called *argumentum ad populum*. Consensus among people who've actually examined the evidence is meaningful. Consensus among people repeating each other is just an echo, and from the outside the two look identical.

**The legitimate version:** popularity is real evidence about some things—that a tool has a community, that a restaurant isn't poisoning people. It's evidence about the crowd's experience, not about facts the crowd never checked.

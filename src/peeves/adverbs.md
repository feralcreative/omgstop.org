## The test

**Ask what the word is describing: the thing, or the doing.**

Describing the thing is an adjective—*a quiet room*, *a careful driver*. Describing the doing is an adverb—*speaks quietly*, *drives carefully*. Same word, different job, different form.

*He sings beautiful* fails the test instantly, because *beautiful* has nothing to attach to except him, and that is not what you meant.

## Before you argue

You are about to say *drive safe*, and you are not entirely wrong. Three real exceptions, and none of them is a general licence.

**Flat adverbs exist.** A handful of words have worked as both since long before anyone was mad about it: *drive fast*, *work hard*, *go slow*, *sit tight*, *hold still*, *aim straight*. Adding *-ly* to those sounds worse, and in a few cases means something else—*hardly working* is not *working hard*. This list is closed. You cannot extend it to *speak clear* because you like how it sounds.

**Imperatives get a pass out loud.** *Drive safe* and *sleep tight* are fossilized as spoken goodbyes and nobody will blink. Put *drive safe* in a written sentence with a subject and a tense, and it stops being a goodbye.

**Think different was a slogan.** It works because it copies the shape of *think big*, where the word is the thing being thought rather than a description of the thinking. It was written that way on purpose by people who were paid well to make you notice. You are not doing that in a status update.

## The other direction is worse

*I feel badly* is the hypercorrection, and it means your fingers are numb.

After *be*, *feel*, *seem*, *look*, *smell*, *sound*, and *taste*, the word points back at the subject, not at the verb—so it takes the adjective. *I feel bad. It looks great. That smells terrible. She seems happy.* Nobody says *the milk smells badly*, because the milk is not the one doing the smelling, and *I feel badly* is the same sentence with the same problem.

Reaching for *-ly* to sound more careful is how you end up sounding less careful. Pick the word that does the job.

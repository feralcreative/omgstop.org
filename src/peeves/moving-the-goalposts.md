---
title: "moving the goalposts"
category: Logical fallacies
order: 118
summary: "You met the standard. They raised it."
rule: "If the bar moves every time it's cleared, it was never a bar."
snark: "That's not what you asked for. It's what you're asking for now."
examples:
  - wrong: "\"Show me one benchmark.\" \"Here.\" \"Well, one benchmark proves nothing.\""
    right: "\"What would actually change your mind? Say it now.\""
    note: "The original request was satisfied, so a new one appeared."
---

The tell is that the new requirement arrives *after* the old one was met, and would have been easy to state at the start. That timing is the whole fallacy.

**How to answer it:** get the standard specified in advance, in writing if it matters. Then when it moves, the movement is visible to everyone.

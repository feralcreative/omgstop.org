---
title: "slippery slope"
category: Logical fallacies
order: 103
summary: "You skipped from a small step to catastrophe and called it a consequence."
rule: "Each step has to actually <em>cause</em> the next one. Show the steps."
snark: "Let this one go and you are inventing your own grammar by Friday."
examples:
  - wrong: "\"If we allow remote Fridays, nobody will ever come in again.\""
    right: "\"What would make Fridays spread to the rest of the week?\""
    note: "The first step doesn't produce the last one on its own. The middle is missing."
---

The trick is momentum: a chain of plausible-sounding steps where each link is never actually justified, arriving somewhere alarming enough that you argue about the destination instead of the reasoning.

**The legitimate version:** slopes are sometimes real, and precedent is a genuine thing. If someone can show the mechanism—why this step *makes* the next one happen—it stops being a fallacy and starts being an argument.

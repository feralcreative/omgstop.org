## Where it comes from

Latin, **for this**. An ad hoc committee is convened for one job and then dissolved; an ad hoc fix is one that solves the case in front of you and generalizes to nothing.

That is the useful part of the phrase, and it is the part people drop when they use it to mean "improvised." Improvised is about how carefully it was made. **Ad hoc** is about how narrowly it applies.

## Why nothing catches it

**Hock** is a real English word. It is a joint on an animal's hind leg, and it is where your watch goes when you pawn it. Spellcheck therefore has no opinion, and *add hock* sails into the meeting notes unchallenged.

## In fairness

Hyphenating it as a modifier—*an ad-hoc process*—is a defensible style call, and you will see it in print from people who know exactly what they are doing.

*Add hock* is not a style call.

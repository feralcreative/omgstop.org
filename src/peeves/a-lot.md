## The rule

**A lot is two words.** Always, in every register, with no exceptions. *Alot* is not in any dictionary because it is not a word.

If you need a test: you would not write *abunch*, *afew*, or *alittle*.

## Allot is a real word, just not that one

**Allot** means to give out a share of something. *We allotted three hours. Check your allotted seat.* Two Ls, and it needs an object—you allot something to someone.

The two are genuinely related, which is why this feels slipperier than it should. A **lot** was originally a portion decided by casting lots, which is where **lottery** comes from too. **Allot** is that same word with a prefix on the front: to divide by lot.

## In fairness

The instinct that it ought to be one word is not stupid. **Already**, **altogether**, and **always** all started as two words and fused. English simply has not done it to *a lot* yet, and until it does, one word is wrong.

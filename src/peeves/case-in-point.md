## The phrase

*In point* is an older English construction meaning "relevant" or "to the purpose"—it survives in *case in point* and in the legal phrase *in point of fact*.

So a **case in point** is a relevant example. **Case and point** would be two separate things, which is not what anyone means and not a phrase.

Spoken quickly, *in* and *and* are almost indistinguishable, which is how this one spreads.

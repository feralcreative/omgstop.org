---
title: "correlation is not causation"
category: Logical fallacies
order: 111
summary: "Two things move together. That is where your evidence stops."
rule: "A and B moving together means A causes B, <em>or</em> B causes A, or C causes both, or it's noise."
snark: "Ice cream sales and drownings. Both go up in July."
aliases:
  - cum-hoc
examples:
  - wrong: "\"Teams using this tool ship faster, so adopt it.\""
    right: "\"Do fast teams adopt it, or does it make teams fast?\""
    note: "The arrow may point the other way. Good teams pick good tools."
---

Four things can produce a correlation: A causes B, B causes A, something else causes both, or coincidence. The data alone can't tell you which, and picking the flattering one isn't analysis.

**How to answer it:** ask for the mechanism, or for what happened when someone changed A on purpose. That's the whole reason controlled experiments exist.

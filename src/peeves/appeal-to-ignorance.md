---
title: "appeal to ignorance"
category: Logical fallacies
order: 108
summary: "Nobody has disproved it. That is not the same as it being true."
rule: "The burden of proof is on whoever makes the claim. Not on everyone else to refute it."
snark: "Now go ask who carries the burden."
aliases:
  - burden-of-proof
examples:
  - wrong: "\"No one's shown it's harmful, so it's safe.\""
    right: "\"Has anyone actually tested it?\""
    note: "Absence of evidence isn't evidence of absence—unless somebody looked."
---

A claim with no evidence for it is not thereby supported. Unfalsifiable claims are especially good at this: if nothing could ever count against it, "you can't disprove it" is a feature of the claim, not a point in its favour.

**The legitimate version:** if someone has genuinely looked, hard, in the place the thing should be, then not finding it *is* evidence. That's how drug trials work. The difference is whether anyone searched.

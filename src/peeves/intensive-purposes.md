## Where it comes from

The phrase is roughly 500 years old and started life in English law as *to all intents, constructions, and purposes*. It piles up near-synonyms on purpose, the way legal language does, to mean "in every practical sense, whatever the technicality says."

*Intensive* means concentrated or thorough. It has never appeared in the phrase and contributes nothing to it.

## Why it survives

This is an **eggcorn**—a misheard phrase reshaped into something that almost makes sense. Spoken quickly, "intents and" and "intensive" are nearly identical, and "intensive purposes" sounds like it could mean something serious.

It doesn't. It's just the wrong words, and in writing there's nowhere to hide.

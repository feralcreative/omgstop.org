## Where it comes from

The piano. The **soft pedal** is the one on the left, and pressing it makes the instrument quieter and more muted. To soft-pedal something is to play it down—the same gesture, applied to bad news.

## Why the wrong one survives

Because **peddle** is a real word and **soft sell** is a real technique, so *soft peddle* turns up sounding like ordinary business vocabulary.

It is the reverse of what you mean. Peddling is pushing a thing at someone until they take it. Soft-pedaling is making the thing smaller so nobody looks at it directly.

The same trap catches **backpedal**, which gets written as *back-peddle* for exactly the same reason. Both of those are feet, not sales.

## In fairness

Keep the hyphen when it is a verb—*soft-pedal the announcement*. And in American English the past tense is **soft-pedaled**, one L.

---
title: "survivorship bias"
category: Logical fallacies
order: 127
summary: "You are only looking at the ones that made it."
rule: "If the failures are invisible, your sample is <em>everyone who succeeded</em>."
snark: "Now go count the ones that did not make it."
examples:
  - wrong: "\"Every founder I've read about took huge risks.\""
    right: "\"What happened to everyone else who took them?\""
    note: "The ones it destroyed didn't get a book deal."
---

The canonical case: WWII analysts studying returning bombers wanted to armour where the bullet holes were. Abraham Wald pointed out those were the survivable hits—armour where the returning planes *weren't* hit, because planes hit there didn't come back.

**How to answer it:** ask where the failures went and whether anyone counted them. If they're not in the data, the data isn't answering the question.

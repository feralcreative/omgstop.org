---
title: "personal incredulity"
category: Logical fallacies
order: 125
summary: "You can't imagine how it works. The universe is not obliged to fit in your head."
rule: "\"I don't understand it\" is a fact about <em>you</em>, not about the claim."
snark: "I cannot see how anyone gets this wrong, so nobody does."
examples:
  - wrong: "\"I can't see how that scales, so it won't.\""
    right: "\"Let's look at how it's meant to scale.\""
    note: "Your inability to picture the mechanism isn't evidence there isn't one."
---

Plenty of true things are deeply unintuitive—quantum mechanics, evolution, most statistics. Difficulty of imagining is a measure of familiarity, not of truth.

**The legitimate version:** if someone can't explain how their thing works when they should be able to, that's a fair reason for suspicion. The problem is treating your own confusion as their refutation.

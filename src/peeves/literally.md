## The problem

*Literally* exists to signal that you are **not** speaking figuratively. Using it as an intensifier on an obvious metaphor spends the only word that does that job.

Once it means "very," there's no word left for the times you need someone to understand you're being precise.

## The fair counterargument

Dictionaries now list the intensifier sense, because it's been in use for centuries and appears in Dickens and Joyce. By the usual standards, that makes it legitimate English.

It's also true that *really*, *truly*, and *very* all took the same path from literal to intensifier, and nobody misses their original meanings.

So this one is a preference, not a rule. But it's a widely held preference, and you'll be told about it.

---
title: "anecdotal evidence"
category: Logical fallacies
order: 113
summary: "Your uncle is not a study."
rule: "One vivid story beats a spreadsheet emotionally. It does not beat it <em>factually</em>."
snark: "Now go find the study."
examples:
  - wrong: "\"My cousin got rich on it, so it works.\""
    right: "\"What happened to everyone else who tried?\""
    note: "You're hearing from the survivor. The rest didn't get a story told about them."
---

Stories are memorable and statistics aren't, so a single anecdote will outweigh a large study in most people's heads. That's a fact about attention, not about evidence.

**The legitimate version:** an anecdote is a fine reason to *investigate* something. One person reporting a side effect is how investigations start. It's just not how they finish.
